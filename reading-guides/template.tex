\makeiliadreadingtitle

Open with one short paragraph that connects the session to the material
participants have already covered and states what they will do next.

\begin{sessionplan}
  \begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Readings (duration).} State the priority order and where to
      find the reading route.
    \item \textbf{Discussion (duration).} State the group size and purpose.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{sessionplan}

\guideheading{Readings}
Explain how to prioritise the readings and how participants can check their
understanding.

\begin{reading}[Main]{Paper title}{https://example.com}
  \begin{readingroute}
    \routeitem{Skim}{Sections to orient the reader.}
    \routeitem{Read}{Sections requiring close attention.}
    \routeitem{Evaluation}{A concrete question that tests the central idea.}
  \end{readingroute}

  \readingoverview{Give just enough context to make the selected sections
  intelligible. Keep claims, qualifications, and the evidence that supports
  them together.}
\end{reading}

\newpage
\begin{reading}[Extension]{Paper title}{https://example.com}
  \begin{readingroute}
    \routeitem{Skim}{Optional orientation sections.}
    \routeitem{Read}{The most relevant extension sections.}
    \routeitem{Evaluation}{A question that exposes the connection to the main
      reading.}
  \end{readingroute}

  \readingoverview{Explain what the extension adds and why it matters for the
  course's argument.}
\end{reading}

\guideheading{Discussion}
Use a small set of open prompts that ask participants to distinguish evidence,
interpretation, limitations, and alternative explanations.
\begin{itemize}
  \item What is the strongest piece of evidence?
  \item What remains uncertain?
  \item What experiment would discriminate between competing explanations?
\end{itemize}
